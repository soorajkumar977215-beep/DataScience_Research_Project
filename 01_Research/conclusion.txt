Conclusion

This study investigated how different levels of missing data affect the performance of basic machine learning models.

The experiments were conducted using the Banknote Authentication dataset and three machine learning models: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest. Missing values were artificially introduced at different levels from 0% to 40%. The models were evaluated using accuracy and F1 score.

The results showed that increasing missing data generally reduced model performance. Logistic Regression showed the largest decrease in mean accuracy, from 98.55% at 0% missing data to 90.47% at 40% missing data. Decision Tree decreased from 99.27% to 93.89%, while Random Forest decreased from 99.64% to 97.96%.

The results also showed that different machine learning models responded differently to missing data. In this experimental setup, Random Forest showed the smallest overall performance decrease.

The study also compared mean and median imputation. The differences between the two methods were generally small at lower missing-data levels. At 40% missing data, median imputation produced higher mean accuracy for all three models.

Overall, the findings indicate that missing data can negatively affect machine learning prediction performance and that the effect can differ between machine learning models. Simple preprocessing methods such as mean and median imputation can help models handle missing values, but their effectiveness may depend on the model and the amount of missing data.
