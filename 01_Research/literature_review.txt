Literature Review

Previous research has investigated the effect of missing data on machine learning prediction and classification.

Marlin (2008) studied learning, inference, and prediction when data contains missing values. The work considered classification with missing features and collaborative prediction with non-random missing data. Different approaches were investigated, including imputation and models that explicitly considered the missing-data process. The study used Yahoo! Music LaunchCast rating data and synthetic data based on the Jester dataset. The work showed that considering the missing-data mechanism can improve prediction performance in some settings.

Radišić, Seljan, and Dunđer (2023) investigated the impact of missing values and missing-data replacement methods on machine learning algorithms. Their study used data from 309 students and compared four versions of the dataset: the original dataset with missing values, arithmetic mean imputation, median imputation, and geometric mean imputation. The study evaluated K-Nearest Neighbors, Naïve Bayes, Random Forest, and Probabilistic Neural Network models using accuracy, F1 score, and confusion matrices. The results showed that the effect of missing-data treatment differed between machine learning algorithms.

These studies show that missing data and its treatment can affect machine learning prediction performance. However, the present study focuses on a controlled experiment in which different percentages of missing data are introduced into the same dataset and the performance of basic machine learning models is measured under the same experimental setup. The study also compares mean and median imputation across different levels of missing data.

Based on this research direction, the present study investigates how model performance changes as the amount of missing data increases and whether simple imputation methods can reduce the effect of missing data on model performance.
