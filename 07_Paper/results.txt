# Results

The repeated experiments showed that increasing the proportion of missing data generally reduced the predictive performance of the machine learning models. However, the magnitude of the reduction varied across models.

At 0% missing data, Logistic Regression achieved a mean accuracy of 98.55%, Decision Tree achieved 99.27%, and Random Forest achieved 99.64%. These results provide the baseline performance for the three models on the selected dataset.

As the proportion of missing data increased to 40%, mean accuracy decreased to 90.84% for Logistic Regression, 95.78% for Decision Tree, and 98.11% for Random Forest.

The overall decrease in mean accuracy from 0% to 40% missing data was 7.71 percentage points for Logistic Regression, 3.49 percentage points for Decision Tree, and 1.53 percentage points for Random Forest. Random Forest exhibited the smallest decrease in mean accuracy in this experimental setup, whereas Logistic Regression exhibited the largest decrease.

The F1-score results showed a similar pattern. At 0% missing data, mean F1-scores were 98.39% for Logistic Regression, 99.19% for Decision Tree, and 99.59% for Random Forest. At 40% missing data, the corresponding mean F1-scores were 89.91%, 95.31%, and 97.90%, respectively. These results indicate that the effect of increasing missingness was also reflected in the balance between precision and recall.

The repeated experiments also showed variation across different missing-data patterns. At 40% missing data, the standard deviation of accuracy was approximately 0.65 percentage points for Logistic Regression, 1.14 percentage points for Decision Tree, and 0.70 percentage points for Random Forest. These values reflect variability across the five random seeds used in the experiment.

Overall, the results suggest that the three models responded differently to increasing levels of missing data. Random Forest maintained comparatively high accuracy and F1-score under the tested conditions, while Logistic Regression showed a greater reduction in both metrics. These findings are limited to the selected dataset, models, missing-data generation procedure, and median imputation method.
