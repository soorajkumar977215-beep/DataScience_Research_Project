Results

The repeated experiments showed that increasing missing data generally reduced the performance of the machine learning models. However, the amount of performance reduction was different for each model.

At 0% missing data, Logistic Regression achieved a mean accuracy of 98.55%, Decision Tree achieved 99.27%, and Random Forest achieved 99.64%.

At 40% missing data, Logistic Regression achieved a mean accuracy of 90.47%, Decision Tree achieved 93.89%, and Random Forest achieved 97.96%.

The overall decrease from 0% to 40% missing data was 8.07 percentage points for Logistic Regression, 5.38 percentage points for Decision Tree, and 1.67 percentage points for Random Forest.

Random Forest showed the smallest overall decrease in mean accuracy in this experimental setup. Logistic Regression showed the largest decrease.

The repeated experiments also showed some variation between different missing-data patterns. At 40% missing data, the standard deviation of accuracy was approximately 1.16% for Logistic Regression, 2.56% for Decision Tree, and 0.71% for Random Forest.

The comparison of mean and median imputation showed that the difference between the two methods was generally small at lower missing-data levels. At 40% missing data, median imputation produced higher mean accuracy than mean imputation for all three models.
