# Conclusion

This study investigated the effect of increasing levels of missing training data on the predictive performance of three machine learning models: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest. The experiments were conducted using the Banknote Authentication dataset, with artificially introduced missing values ranging from 0% to 40%. Model performance was evaluated using accuracy and F1 score.

The results showed that increasing the proportion of missing training data generally reduced predictive performance, although the magnitude of the reduction varied across models. Mean accuracy for Logistic Regression decreased from 98.55% with no artificially introduced missing data to 90.84% at 40% missing data. Decision Tree accuracy decreased from 99.27% to 95.78%, while Random Forest accuracy decreased from 99.64% to 98.11%.

Among the three evaluated models, Random Forest showed the smallest decrease in mean accuracy, at 1.53 percentage points, while Logistic Regression showed the largest decrease, at 7.71 percentage points. These findings suggest that the models differed in their sensitivity to missing training data under the experimental conditions used in this study. However, the results do not establish that the same performance patterns will hold across other datasets or experimental settings.

The repeated experiments used median imputation and five random seeds to examine performance across different missing-data patterns. Although a separate experiment compared mean and median imputation, its findings should be reported independently and only after its results have been verified. The repeated experiment presented in this study does not establish that median imputation is superior to mean imputation.

Overall, this study demonstrates that missing training data can affect machine learning prediction performance differently across models. Future work should investigate additional datasets, missing-data mechanisms, imputation methods, and classification algorithms, as well as different train-test splits. Such experiments would help establish whether the observed patterns generalize to a wider range of machine learning applications.
