# Future Work

Future studies can extend this work in several directions.

First, the experiments can be repeated using multiple datasets from different domains to determine whether similar patterns are observed across different data characteristics, dataset sizes, and classification tasks.

Second, future work can investigate different missing-data mechanisms rather than introducing missing values randomly. For example, experiments could consider Missing Completely at Random (MCAR), Missing at Random (MAR), and Missing Not at Random (MNAR) scenarios to evaluate how different missingness assumptions affect model performance.

Third, additional machine learning models can be evaluated, including K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machines, and other classification algorithms. This would help determine whether the observed differences in robustness to missing data extend to a wider range of models.

Fourth, additional missing-data handling methods can be investigated. These may include KNN imputation, iterative imputation, and multiple imputation. Future experiments could also compare mean and median imputation using repeated trials and consistent evaluation procedures.

Finally, future experiments can use additional evaluation metrics, repeated train-test splits, and cross-validation to provide a more comprehensive assessment of model performance and variability. These extensions could help establish whether the findings remain consistent under different experimental conditions.
