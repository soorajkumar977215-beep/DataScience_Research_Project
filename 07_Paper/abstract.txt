# Abstract

Missing data is a common challenge in real-world datasets and can affect the predictive performance of machine learning models. This study investigates how different levels of missing training data influence the performance of three classification algorithms.

The Banknote Authentication dataset, containing 1,372 observations and four numerical input features, was used in the experiments. Artificial missing values were introduced into the training features at six levels: 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 30%, and 40%. Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest were evaluated using accuracy and F1 score. Median imputation was applied to handle missing values, and five random seeds were used to examine performance across different missing-data patterns. In total, 90 model runs were conducted.

The results showed that increasing the proportion of missing training data generally reduced predictive performance. Mean accuracy for Logistic Regression decreased from 98.55% at 0% missing data to 90.84% at 40% missing data. Decision Tree accuracy decreased from 99.27% to 95.78%, while Random Forest accuracy decreased from 99.64% to 98.11%. Among the three evaluated models, Random Forest showed the smallest decrease in mean accuracy, whereas Logistic Regression showed the largest decrease.

These findings indicate that the effect of missing training data varies across machine learning models. However, the results are limited to the selected dataset, the missing-data generation procedure, the evaluated algorithms, and the imputation method. Further experiments using additional datasets and missing-data handling strategies are needed to assess the generalizability of these findings.
