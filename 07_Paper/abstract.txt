Abstract

Missing data is a common problem in real-world datasets and can affect the performance of machine learning models. This study investigates how different levels of missing data affect the prediction performance of basic machine learning models.

The Banknote Authentication dataset was used in this study. Missing values were artificially introduced into the training data at six levels: 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 30%, and 40%. Three machine learning models were evaluated: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest. Multiple random seeds were used to repeat the missing-data experiments. Model performance was evaluated using accuracy and F1 score. Mean and median imputation were also compared as simple missing-data preprocessing methods.

The results showed that increasing missing data generally reduced model performance. Logistic Regression showed the largest decrease in mean accuracy, from 98.55% at 0% missing data to 90.47% at 40% missing data. Decision Tree decreased from 99.27% to 93.89%, while Random Forest decreased from 99.64% to 97.96%. In this experimental setup, Random Forest showed the smallest overall performance decrease. The comparison of mean and median imputation showed that their differences were generally small at lower missing-data levels, while median imputation produced higher mean accuracy at 40% missing data for all three models.

Overall, the findings indicate that increasing missing data can negatively affect machine learning prediction performance and that different models can respond differently to missing data.
