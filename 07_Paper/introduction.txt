# Introduction

Machine learning is widely used for prediction and classification tasks in many different areas. However, real-world datasets often contain missing values, which can affect the performance of machine learning models. Missing data may occur because of errors during data collection, incomplete records, or other practical limitations.

Handling missing data is an important preprocessing step in machine learning. Simple imputation methods, such as replacing missing values with the mean or median, are commonly used to prepare datasets for model training. However, the impact of missing data may differ across machine learning algorithms, and some models may be more robust to missing information than others.

The main objective of this study is to investigate how different levels of missing data affect the prediction performance of Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest models. The study evaluates model performance at multiple missing-data levels using accuracy and F1-score. Median imputation is used in the main repeated experiment to examine how model performance changes as the proportion of missing data increases. A separate mean-versus-median imputation comparison is considered independently and is not part of the main repeated experiment reported in this paper.
